The load profile is the shape of the test over time: all users from the start, users that arrive little by little, a sudden burst, or a rise and a fall. Choose the shape that looks like the moment you want to test, for example the opening of a sale.

## Example: a ramp up to 20 users

<Steps>
1. In the setup of a performance test, set **Load profile** to **Ramp up**.
2. Leave **Virtual users** in `20` and **Duration** in `1` **min**.
3. Read the sentence under the chart: *5 virtual users for 15 s, ramp up to 20 over 15 s, then hold 20 for 30 s, each executing all requests in order.*
4. Drag the second marker of the chart to the right, until the time under it says `0:40`.
5. The sentence now says *ramp up to 20 over 25 s, then hold 20 for 20 s*.
</Steps>

## Virtual users and duration

| Field | Default | Limits |
|---|---|---|
| **Virtual users** | 20 | From 1 to 500. A larger number becomes 500. |
| **Duration** | 1 **min** | From 1 second to 60 minutes. A longer duration becomes 60 minutes. |

- Next to **Duration**, choose the unit: **sec** or **min**. The arrows of the field go 5 seconds or half a minute at a time.
- The duration is always a whole number of seconds: `1.25` **min** becomes 75 seconds.
- **Virtual users** is the top of the load. With **Fixed**, all of them run from start to end. With the other profiles, only at the top.

## The profiles

| Profile | Shape | Phases by default | The sentence, with 20 users, 1 min and an initial load of 5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fixed** | All users from start to end. | — | *20 virtual users for 1 min, each executing all requests in order.* |
| **Ramp up** | Starts with the initial load, goes up in a straight line to all users and stays there. | Starts going up at 25 % of the time and arrives at 50 %. | *5 virtual users for 15 s, ramp up to 20 over 15 s, then hold 20 for 30 s, each executing all requests in order.* |
| **Spike** | Starts with the initial load, jumps to all users at once and drops back. | The spike goes from 40 % to 60 % of the time. | *5 virtual users for 24 s, spike to 20 for 12 s, then back to 5 for 24 s, each executing all requests in order.* |
| **Peak** | Goes up from the initial load to all users, holds and goes down again. | Arrives at 25 % of the time and starts going down at 75 %. | *Ramp from 5 to 20 virtual users over 15 s, hold 20 for 30 s, then ramp down to 5 over 15 s, each executing all requests in order.* |

In a test of workflows, the sentence ends with *each executing the happy path of the selected workflows.*

Choosing another profile puts its phases back to their defaults.

### Which one to choose

- **Fixed**: a steady load, to measure normal times.
- **Ramp up**: to see at which number of users the times start to grow.
- **Spike**: a sudden burst, like a sale that opens at a fixed hour, and whether the API recovers after it.
- **Peak**: a busy hour that comes and goes.

## Initial load

**Initial load (users)** appears for **Ramp up**, **Spike** and **Peak**. It is how many users there are at the start, and also at the end of **Spike** and **Peak**.

- It starts at 5.
- It goes from 1 to **Virtual users**. If you lower **Virtual users** below it, it goes down with it.

## The preview

The chart under the fields draws the shape: virtual users over time.

- On the left, the number of users at the top, for example **20 VUs**, and the initial load, for example **5 VUs**, when there is room for both.
- At the bottom, `0` and the duration, for example **1 min**.
- Under the chart, the sentence tells the same in words.

## Move the phases

**Ramp up**, **Spike** and **Peak** have two markers on the chart, one where each phase begins and one where it ends. The hint under the chart says *Drag the markers (or use the arrow keys) to move each phase.*

<Steps>
1. Press on a marker: its handle at the top, or its line.
2. Drag it left or right. The time appears under it, as `minutes:seconds`, for example `0:40`.
3. Release. The shape and the sentence change.
</Steps>

![Set up your performance test with Load profile Peak, 8 virtual users and 24 sec: the preview from 1 to 8 VUs with two markers, the sentence Ramp from 1 to 8 virtual users over 6 s, hold 8 for 12 s, then ramp down to 1 over 6 s, Initial load (users) 1 and the hint about dragging the markers](/media/en/runner-perf-setup.webp)

- The markers move in whole seconds.
- A marker cannot cross the other one: there is always at least one second between them.

### With the keyboard

<Steps>
1. Press Tab until a marker has the focus. Screen readers call them **Phase start** and **Phase end**.
2. Press → or ← to move it one second.
3. Hold Shift to move it ten seconds at a time.
</Steps>

## Pause between iterations

**Pause between iterations (ms)** is how long each user waits after an iteration before starting the next one. It is 0 by default: each user starts again at once. The arrows go 100 ms at a time.

With a pause, each user sends fewer requests per second, closer to how a person uses an app. With 0, the test sends as many as your API and your computer allow.

## How users come and go

- When the profile adds users, the new ones start their first iteration.
- When the profile removes users, each one ends the iteration it is in, then waits.
- When the time is up, every user stops, and the requests on their way are cut and not counted.
