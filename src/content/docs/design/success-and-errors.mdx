Every step needs two decisions: which response means it went well, and what to do when the API answers with an error. Luvin sets the first from the contract and leaves the second to you. Both are in the step panel, and the second is also on the card of the step.

This page is the overview. For how a run moves from step to step after each action, read [Error branches](/design/branches/).

## Example: a Pokémon that does not exist

In **Pokemon card**, the first step ends the workflow when the API answers 404, so "the Pokémon does not exist" is a known outcome and not a crash.

<Tabs>
  <TabItem label="In the app">

  In the step panel of `getPokemon`, **It's a success if it responds** has **200** selected. Under **If it responds with an error**, the list next to **404** reads **End the workflow**. On the canvas, with **Actions by response** on, the card shows the row `404 → pokemonNotFound` **end ✕**.

  ![The step panel with the outputs of a step and the action chosen for each documented error](/media/en/step-outputs.webp)

  </TabItem>
  <TabItem label="In the YAML">

```yaml title="pokemon.arazzo.yaml" {7-11}
- stepId: getPokemon
  successCriteria:
    - condition: $statusCode == 200
    - context: $response.body
      condition: $.abilities[?(@.is_hidden == false)]
      type: jsonpath
  onFailure:
    - name: pokemonNotFound
      type: end
      criteria:
        - condition: $statusCode == 404
```

  </TabItem>
</Tabs>

## What counts as success

<Steps>
1. In the **Design** tab, click a step.
2. Find **It's a success if it responds**. It has one option per 2xx status the contract documents for the operation.
3. Click the status you expect.
</Steps>

A new step starts with the lowest documented 2xx, or 200 if the contract documents none. In the file this is written as `$statusCode == 200`. Any other response makes the step fail.

### More than the status

A step can also check the content of the response. These extra criteria are written in the [YAML tab](/design/arazzo-editor/). The panel lists them below the statuses with the link **Edit in the YAML**, and the card shows them under **Success criteria**. All the criteria must hold at once.

In **Pokemon card**, the step `getSpecies` checks the status and a field:

```yaml title="pokemon.arazzo.yaml" {3}
successCriteria:
  - condition: $statusCode == 200
  - condition: $response.body#/capture_rate >= 0
```

See [JSONPath and regex criteria](/recipes/criteria/) for runnable examples.

![The panel of getSpecies: the 200 status selected, the extra criterion $response.body#/capture_rate >= 0 and the link Edit in the YAML](/media/en/design-criteria-panel.webp)

## What happens on each error

**If it responds with an error** lists every error status the contract documents for the operation, with the contract's description. Each one has a list of actions.

| Action | What the workflow does |
|---|---|
| **No action (the workflow fails)** | The default. The step fails and the workflow stops with a generic failure. |
| **End the workflow** | Stops at this step as a known outcome, for example "the Pokémon does not exist". |
| **Retry (up to 3 times)** | Calls the step again, up to 3 times, waiting 1 second before each try. If it still fails, the workflow fails. |
| **Go to “step”** | Jumps to another step of the workflow and continues from there. |

You can choose the action from the card on the canvas or from the step panel. Both write the same thing in the file.

### From the card

In the **Design** tab, each card has an **On failure** section with one row per error status the contract documents. **Actions by response**, above the canvas, must be on to see the rows. It is on by default.

| The row shows | Button at the end of the row |
|---|---|
| `404 → no action` **fails** | **+**. Its tooltip says **What to do if it responds 404**. |
| `404 → on404` **end ✕**, or another action | A pencil. Its tooltip says **Change what to do if it responds 404**. |

Click the button and choose in the menu **If it responds 404**: **+ A new step…**, **Go to “Get weakness”** (only later steps), **End the workflow**, **Retry (up to 3 times)** or, when the status already has an action, **Remove the branch**. For a status the contract does not document, use **+ If it responds another status…** at the end of the section.

Each option is explained in [The branch menu](/design/branch-menu/). Statuses such as 429 or 503 are in [Statuses the contract doesn't document](/design/branch-other-status/).

### From the step panel

<Steps>
1. Click the step on the canvas. Its panel opens on the right.
2. Under **If it responds with an error**, open the list next to the status, for example **404**.
3. Choose the action.
</Steps>

![The section If it responds with an error of createOrder: 400 with no action, 401 End the workflow, 409 Go to “List products” and 429 Retry (up to 3 times)](/media/en/design-error-actions.webp)

<video controls muted playsinline preload="metadata" poster="/media/en/design-errors.webp" src="/media/en/design-errors.mp4"></video>

Luvin writes it in the file with the name `on404`:

```yaml
onFailure:
  - name: on404
    type: end
    criteria:
      - condition: $statusCode == 404
```

Every action you set also gives you a test: Luvin generates a case that simulates that response and checks that the action fires. See [Cases](/test/cases/).

<Aside type="tip" title="Change the retry limit">
**Retry** writes `retryLimit: 3` and `retryAfter: 1` (seconds). To use other numbers, change them in the YAML tab, or save the action as a [reusable](/design/reusables/): its panel has both fields.
</Aside>

Two more things can show in this section:

- A **Reusables** group at the top of the list, when a saved action handles that status. Choosing it applies the reusable.
- The line **There are 2 error action(s) with custom criteria.** with **Edit in the YAML**. Those actions do not depend on a single status, for example they depend on a field of the response. The panel does not edit them.

### A new step for an error

Sometimes an error needs its own call, for example to list Pokémon when the species is not found. **+ A new step…**, in the menu of the card, opens a dialog with the palette, titled **New step if it responds 404**. The step you pick goes at the end of the workflow, the error jumps to it with an action `on404` of type `goto`, and the last step of the happy path gets an action on success named `done` that ends the run before the new step.

[A new step for an error](/design/branch-new-step/) shows the whole flow, the YAML before and after, several branch steps and how to undo it. To add a step in another place of the workflow, use the **+** on the arrows between steps: see [Insert a step](/design/insert-step/).

Runnable examples: [Retry, jump and end on errors](/recipes/error-branches/) and [Prove the API rejects bad data](/recipes/expected-error/).

## Retries and slow calls

### What a retry does

When a step does not succeed and its action is **Retry**, Luvin sends the step again. Each try appears in the results as, for example, **Retry 1 of 3 (action "on429")**. If the last try fails too, the results say **The 3 retries were exhausted** and the step fails.

| Setting | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| How many times | `retryLimit`. If an action has none, Luvin retries once. |
| How long to wait | `retryAfter`, in seconds, between tries. |
| Real API limit | <Badge text="Max 10 s" variant="caution" /> Luvin never waits more than 10 seconds between two tries, even if `retryAfter` is larger. |
| Simulation | It does not wait at all. |
| Where the wait comes from | Only from `retryAfter`. Luvin does not read a `Retry-After` header of the response. |

### Run another step or workflow before the retry

A `retry` action can also name something to run first. Luvin runs it once, then calls the failed step again. Use it to renew a token: the call answers 401, a login runs, the call is tried again.

<Tabs>
  <TabItem label="Step: stepId">

```yaml title="retry-step.arazzo.yaml" {5-6}
onFailure:
  - name: loginAndRetry
    type: retry
    stepId: login
    retryLimit: 2
    criteria:
      - condition: $statusCode == 401
```

Luvin runs the step `login` of the same workflow. The step must exist: if not, the analysis says **Action "loginAndRetry" (retry) points to the non-existent step "login".**

  </TabItem>
  <TabItem label="Workflow: workflowId">

```yaml {4}
onFailure:
  - name: renewToken
    type: retry
    workflowId: $sourceDescriptions.auth.login
    retryLimit: 2
    criteria:
      - condition: $statusCode == 401
```

Luvin runs the workflow, in this file or in another Arazzo file, and stores its outputs so the retried step can read them.

  </TabItem>
</Tabs>

In the results, the try shows as **Retry 1 of 2: runs step "login" first (action "loginAndRetry")**. The step or workflow that runs first counts toward `retryLimit`, and Luvin waits `retryAfter` before each retry.

Runnable example: [Log in again and retry](/recipes/retry-step/).

### Cut a slow call with `timeout`

A step can carry a `timeout`, in milliseconds. If the API does not answer in that time, Luvin cuts the call and the step fails with **The API did not answer within 3000 ms (step timeout).** The step panel has no field for it. Write it in the [YAML tab](/design/arazzo-editor/), inside the step:

```yaml
steps:
  - stepId: getOrder
    operationId: $sourceDescriptions.shop.getOrder
    timeout: 3000
```

See [Failure reasons](/reference/failure-reasons/) for how to read the message. For a step that waits for a message, `timeout` means something else: see [Messaging steps](/design/messaging/).

## See the actions on the canvas

Turn on **Actions by response**, next to **Show:** above the canvas. Each card gets a section, **On failure**, with one row per response:

| Row | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `404 → pokemonNotFound` **end ✕** | On a 404, the action `pokemonNotFound` ends the workflow. |
| `429 → on429` **retries ×3** | On a 429, the step is tried again up to 3 times. |
| `409 → on409` **go to getOrder** | On a 409, the run jumps to the step `getOrder`. |
| `500 → no action` **fails** | The contract documents a 500 and nothing handles it. |

A jump is also drawn as an orange dashed line that leaves the row, runs along the left of the cards and enters the target step. Its label is the status and the name of the action. In the legend, the orange dot is **jumps to another step**. To move a jump line away from other lines, drag its vertical part to the side; the pointer shows **Drag to move the line**. Data lines move the same way.

Turn the switch off to hide the section and the jump lines when you want to read only the data.

![The canvas with Actions by response on: the 409 row of createOrder jumps to listProducts and an orange dashed line runs along the left up to that step](/media/en/design-jump-line.webp)

## Check that every response is handled

The **Overview** tab of the workflow has a section, **Response coverage**, that compares the contract with the workflow.

<Steps>
1. Open the **Overview** tab.
2. Open **Response coverage**. Its badge reads, for example, **5/7 handled**.
</Steps>

The table has one row per step and documented status:

| Handling in the Arazzo | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ✓ **Success** | The status is the one the step expects. |
| ✓ **Action** | An action handles it. The name of the action follows. |
| ✕ **Unhandled** | Nothing handles it. If the API answers this, the workflow fails with a generic error. |

You do not have to handle everything. Handle the errors your application treats differently, such as "not found" or "not approved".

![Response coverage with 7/11 handled: one row per step and status, with Success, Action or Unhandled](/media/en/design-coverage.webp)
